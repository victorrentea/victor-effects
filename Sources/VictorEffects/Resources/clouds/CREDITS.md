# Cloud sprites

Cut out of two photographs, both usable without attribution; credited anyway
because a public repo should say where its pixels came from.

| sprite | source | licence |
|---|---|---|
| `cloud-0`, `cloud-2`, `cloud-5` | [Single cloud on sky.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Single_cloud_on_sky.jpg) | **Public domain** |
| `cloud-1`, `cloud-3`, `cloud-4`, `cloud-6` | [The sky is beautiful with cumulus clouds.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_sky_is_beautiful_with_cumulus_clouds.jpg) | **CC0** |

Two cumulus forms across seven sprites — mirrored, horizontally stretched, and
in one case a tighter crop — arranged so no two neighbours in the band share a
form and a mirror never sits beside its original. At band size, with the clouds
overlapping and half of each one above the screen edge, a stretch reads as a
different cloud far more than a different crop of the same one does.

**Two and not seven, because clean separability is rare.** The matte is keyed
off the SKY, so a photograph only works if its sky is a saturated, even blue
right up to the cloud's edge. Most cloud photographs are hazy near their
subject — pale blue against white cloud is no key at all — and a scored sweep of
about fifty public-domain candidates produced exactly these two plus one cloud
too small to use. The screening and the pipeline are described in
`docs/overlay-effects.md` under ⛈️ Storm.

The clip is public domain too: *Rain and thunder*, David Öhlin, 2006.
